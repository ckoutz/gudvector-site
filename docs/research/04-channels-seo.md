# Güd Vector — marketing channels and SEO / AI-search brief

**For:** Güd Vector Consulting Services (gudvector.com), Concord / San Francisco Bay Area  
**Offer:** Custom websites + custom AI for local service businesses (trades, home services, inspection, environmental testing). Quotes require owner approval. Customer portal. Scheduling.  
**Constraint:** Owner-operator budget. Not a HubSpot-enterprise mix.  
**Written:** 2 Sep 2026, ~6:30 PM PT  
**Rule:** No invented volumes or conversion rates. Where a figure is not published, it is marked **unknown**. Sources are listed at the end of each channel and again in §4.

**How this brief is scored.** Rank is “likely ROI for *this* firm selling *this* offer to *this* buyer,” not “best marketing in general.” The buyer is a busy shop owner (often Spanish-speaking crew + English office, or a one-person truck) who is not sitting on LinkedIn hunting for a web agency. They notice a problem when: a neighbor’s site looks better, Angi/Google is sending jobs they cannot convert, a CPA says the books are a mess because quotes live in text threads, or someone they trust names a vendor.

**Existing pipeline this brief evaluates:** Concord + immediate East Bay landscaping/cleaning finder, generated 24 Aug 2026. Landscaping: 6 qualified / 24 skipped as decent-or-modern. Cleaning: 6 qualified / 18 skipped. Mock outreach already exists for AB Gardening and Lopez Landscaping. No business in those lists was contacted as of the finder notes.

### 1. Direct outreach to businesses with weak or no websites — **#1 ROI**

**Horizon:** Quick win (pipeline already exists).  
**Effort:** Medium. The finder work is done for two trades in one geo; expanding it is the ongoing cost.  
**Cash:** Near zero. Phone, a one-page PDF or live preview, and time.

**What you already have (evaluate, do not rebuild):**

- Landscaping / lawn / tree, Concord + Pleasant Hill / Martinez / Walnut Creek / Clayton / Pittsburg / Pacheco / Bay Point: **6 qualified, 24 skipped** after a public website check (generated 24 Aug 2026). Qualified examples include Palomas Landscaping (no owned domain found; 31 years, license #903764), Apex Tree Care (no Concord-owned domain; 155 Yelp reviews), AB Gardening (no owned domain; 30+ years; Angi 4.9 / 78 reviews), G&O Lawn Care (unfinished template with placeholder phone). Drafts already exist for AB Gardening and Lopez Landscaping.
- Cleaning, same geo: **6 qualified, 18 skipped**. Examples: VIP Cleaning (owned domain 404s; live site is a Wix subdomain still showing a builder banner), Imperial Janitorial (no owned domain; BBB A+ since 2012), Queen’s Housecleaning (1990s table HTML).

**Density:** In this slice, roughly **1 in 5** publicly checked landscapers and cleaners still have no site or a clearly weak one. That is a real local inventory, not a theory.

**Why this buyer is here:** They are **not** searching “custom website for landscaper Concord.” They are on a job. BrightLocal’s 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, and **54% visit the business’s website after reading positive reviews** — up from 32% when BrightLocal last asked in 2019. BrightLocal also notes **only 40% of local businesses have a dedicated website**. So the shop that is strong on Angi/Yelp and invisible on its own domain is leaking the exact moment the homeowner tries to confirm them. That is the pitch. It is specific, visual, and does not require the owner to “go look at software.”

**Tactics that match the existing drafts (not a new playbook):**

- One shop at a time. Screenshot or live preview of *their* Google/Angi presence vs. a mobile-first page with click-to-call, real photos, and a quote flow that still needs **owner approval**.
- Phone first when a number is public; email second (many of these addresses are Hotmail/Yahoo — they do get opened by owners).
- Do not batch-mail the 12. A 12-name list this qualified is a two-week calling block, not a sequence.

**Sourced reason it works for *this* buyer:** The offer is custom and quoted. That already requires a conversation. Buying intent for “new website + quoting that I don’t have to learn” shows up as a broken Wix banner or a missing domain, not as a Google Ads click. Nielsen-style “people hire who they already know” still holds; the finder just tells you who to call. Cold-email industry reply rates of **2–5%** (SalesTarget / Instantly 2025–2026 platform averages) are what you get from strangers. A list of six landscapers who have no website, with a preview of *their* shop, is a different channel.

**Why it can fail:** Volume is finite. Concord + neighbors is a few dozen qualified names per trade, not a thousand. After the first pass, you expand geo (Pittsburg → Antioch → Oakland hills) or trade (inspectors, environmental testing, plumbers) — you do not “scale the sequence.”

**Sources:** `/workspace/leads-landscaping.md`, `/workspace/leads-cleaning.md`; BrightLocal LCRS 2026, https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/; SalesTarget Multichannel Outreach Benchmarks 2026; Instantly platform-wide reply rate cited in Martal, “B2B Cold Email Statistics 2026.”

### 2. Referrals / partner channel — **#2 ROI**

**Horizon:** Long-term, but first conversations this month.  
**Effort:** Medium-low. A short partner one-pager + 8–12 coffees with people who already sit across from shop owners.  
**Cash:** $0–a few hundred (coffee, a printed leave-behind). No software required.

**Who actually sits with this buyer:**

- **Bookkeepers and small-firm CPAs** in Concord / Walnut Creek / Pleasant Hill — they see quotes living in text threads, deposits untracked, and “I think we made money this year.”
- **Insurance agents** writing GL / workers’ comp / auto for C-27, C-36, C-20 shops — they already ask for certificates, job lists, and “are you licensed and looking professional.”
- **Other trades** on the same job (landscaper ↔ hardscape ↔ irrigation; inspector ↔ environmental tester ↔ plumber).
- **Not** other web agencies.

**Why this buyer is there:** Shop owners hire vendors the way they hire subs — a name from someone they already pay. LinkedIn commentary from accounting coaches in 2026 restates the same pattern: referrals are the default, and the useful partners are the people who serve the client *before* they need a website (bookkeeper, insurance, another trade), not a competing marketer. Paid “partner programs” in adjacent verticals (bookkeeping, insurance) exist because those professions already make introductions; Güd Vector does not need a commission engine to copy that motion. A $200 thank-you or a reciprocal intro is enough at this size.

**Tactics:**

- One sentence the partner can say: “If their site is a Wix banner or they have no site, and they hate software, I know a Concord shop that builds the site plus quoting they approve themselves.”
- Give the partner a URL they can survive looking up. Right now gudvector.com is a 3-page site with **no industry proof pages** (see 01-site-audit.md). A partner will Google you. That is why channel #3 and #5 exist — to not embarrass the introducer.
- Start with 3 bookkeepers + 2 insurance producers + 2 trades who already like the work. Do not build a “program.”

**Sourced reason it works / doesn’t:** Works because trust substitutes for search. Does not work if Güd Vector has no named local examples and no GBP/reviews for the partner to check (BrightLocal 2026: 47% of consumers won’t use a business with fewer than 20 reviews; 31% require 4.5+ stars — and partners behave like cautious consumers when they stake their name).

**Sources:** BrightLocal LCRS 2026; CREIA/ASHI/PHCC/CLCA as rooms where those partners already gather (see channel 4).

### 3. Local SEO / Google Business Profile for Güd Vector itself — **#3 ROI**

**Horizon:** Quick win to complete; long-term to rank against Bay Area agencies.  
**Effort:** 2–4 hours to complete + 20–40 min/week (posts, photos, review replies).  
**Cash:** $0. Optional later: a local listing tool in the $40–$80/mo band Resourcera cites for single-location businesses — not required on day one.

**What the public data actually says (not agency folklore):**

- Google has long stated that a **complete** Business Profile gets on average **7× more clicks** than an incomplete one. BrightLocal’s GMB Insights study quotes this directly (“Google even states…”). Aggregators in 2026 still cite Google for 7× clicks and **70% more in-store visits**.
- BrightLocal **SMB Marketing 2025** (cited in LCRS 2026): **only 35% of SMBs have a Google Business Profile**.
- BrightLocal LCRS 2026 (1,002 US adults, published 11 Feb 2026): **97%** read reviews; Google’s share of review-reading fell **83% → 71%**; **AI tools for local recommendations rose 6% → 45%** and are now the **#3** discovery source behind Google and Facebook; **54%** visit the website after positive reviews; **47%** won’t use a business with fewer than 20 reviews; **31%** require 4.5+ stars; **80%** more likely to use a business that replies to every review.
- Google Local Pack ranking is still dominated by GBP signals in practitioner studies (Whitespark / local-factor roundups commonly put GBP in the ~32–36% band). Treat the exact percentage as practitioner estimate, not a Google-published weight.

**Why this buyer (the contractor) is *sometimes* here:** They are not living in the Local Pack for “web design Concord.” They *are* there when (a) a partner Googles “Güd Vector,” (b) they type “website for my plumbing company” once, late, after a bad month, (c) ChatGPT/Gemini/Perplexity answers “who builds contractor websites in the East Bay.” BrightLocal’s AI jump is the part that matters for a tiny firm: **entity + reviews + a clear service description** are what LLMs scrape.

**Tactics (cheap, specific):**

- Claim/verify GBP. Categories that match the actual offer: something in **Website designer** / **Internet marketing service** / **Software company** — pick the primary that Google allows, do not stuff “plumber.” Service area: Concord, Contra Costa, East Bay, SF Bay Area. No fake storefront.
- Description ≥ a few hundred characters, in plain language: custom websites for trades and home services; quoting the owner approves; customer portal; scheduling. Concord, CA.
- Photos: real work (the AB Gardening / Lopez previews, portal screens, mobile pages) — not stock.
- Review engine: every paying customer, asked once, no incentive (FTC Consumer Review Rule; Google/Yelp ban paid-for-positive). 20 reviews is the BrightLocal threshold almost half of people use.
- NAP consistency: gudvector.com, GBP, any chamber listing. The live site schema currently names “Güd Vector Consulting Services,” email info@gudvector.com, areaServed San Francisco Bay Area (01-site-audit.md). Keep that string stable so AI does not split the entity.
- Weekly GBP post is optional; **replying to reviews** is not.

**Sourced reason it works:** Free, compounding, and it is the lookup layer for every other channel.  
**Sourced reason it is not #1:** The ICP rarely has local-pack intent for *your* category. A complete GBP will not fill a week with inbound plumber-website leads in Concord. It will make the leads from #1 and #2 not bounce off a thin listing.

**Sources:** Google (complete-profile 7× clicks, via BrightLocal GMB Insights Study https://www.brightlocal.com/research/google-my-business-insights-study/ and Google Business Profile Help); BrightLocal LCRS 2026; BrightLocal SMB Marketing 2025 (35% figure, cited on the LCRS page); Resourcera local SEO tool cost band.

**Horizon:** Long-term (first useful citations in weeks if structure is clean; rankings in months).  
**Effort:** High to do well. 8–12 pages below are a quarter of writing, not a weekend.  
**Cash:** $0 if the owner writes from real jobs. A freelancer for polish is optional.

**Why this is on the short list despite being slow:**  
gudvector.com currently has **three indexable URLs** (`/`, `/privacy`, `/terms`). No `/blog`, `/industries`, comparisons, or FAQs (01-site-audit.md, 2 Sep 2026). Google and LLMs have almost nothing extractable except a homepage paragraph. BrightLocal 2026: 45% of consumers used AI for local recommendations. GEO/AEO practitioner writeups (Discovered Labs, Geodocs, Brand Armor, 2026) consistently describe the same citable pattern: **named entity, answer-first, FAQ, comparison tables, dates, primary-source prices**. That is cheap to implement and is also the leave-behind for channels 1–4.

**Do not start a general blog.** Do the 8–12 pages in §2.

### 6. Cold email / LinkedIn to owners — **execution of #1, poor as a volume channel**

**Horizon:** Quick if it is the Concord list. Slow/negative if it is purchased data.  
**Effort:** Medium.  
**Cash:** $0 with Gmail + phone. Instantly Growth ~$47/mo or Smartlead Base ~$39/mo if you ever need warmup; **do not buy this until the 12-name list is worked by hand.** LinkedIn Sales Navigator Core: **$119.99/mo** or **$89.99/mo annual** ($1,079.88/yr) as of 2026 third-party recaps of LinkedIn’s published plans.

**Benchmarks (platform averages, not Güd Vector results):**

- Cold email reply: **~2–5%** average; Instantly platform-wide **3.43%** (2025–2026); “good” often cited as 5%+.
- LinkedIn connection accept: **25–35%** average with a note; DM to connections **10–15%** (SalesTarget 2026).
- LinkedIn InMail: often cited **10–25%**, with 50 InMails/mo on Sales Navigator Core.

**Why this buyer is weakly on LinkedIn:** Many Concord landscaping/cleaning owners run on Yelp, Angi, a Hotmail address, and a cell phone (see the finder files). LinkedIn is more useful for **partners** (CPAs, insurance) than for C-27 owners. A connection request to “Miguel at Palomas Landscaping” may never be seen; a call to (925) 382-8293 might.

**Sourced reason the *personalized* version works:** Same as channel 1 — the artifact is “I looked at your missing/broken site.”  
**Sourced reason the *volume* version does not:** 2–5% of a rented list of “California contractors” is noise, spam-folder risk, and a brand problem in a small East Bay pond. CAN-SPAM still applies; bought lists of trades are messy.

**Skip Sales Navigator until** the partner channel needs search filters. A free LinkedIn account is enough to look up a CPA.

### 9. Google Ads and Local Services Ads — **do not spend here yet**

**Horizon:** Would be quick *if* economics worked. They likely do not at owner-operator budget.  
**Effort:** High to avoid lighting money on fire.  
**Cash:** WordStream 2026 (13,000+ US search campaigns, Apr 2025–Mar 2026): **average CPC $5.42**, **average CPL $66.69**, suggested SMB starting budget **$1,000–$2,500/month**. Category medians from the same report: **Business Services CPC $5.87** (conversion rate 4.85% in the Little Dragon recap of WordStream); **Home & Home Improvement CPC $8.33**.

**Closer to Güd Vector’s actual auction:**

- **“Website design / web design / software” is not Home Services.** PipeRocket’s Aug 2026 B2B SaaS sample: **non-brand search CPC $13.75**, **CPL $207**; brand CPC $3.12 / CPL $34; blended CPC $6.81 / CPL $84.
- FoundryCRO 2026 roundup: B2B SaaS non-brand often **$8.50–$14.00** CPC.
- Keyword-level CPC for **“contractor website,” “field service software,” “Jobber alternative”**: **unknown** (no public Keyword Planner dump found for those exact strings in this research). Treat them as *at least* Business Services / SaaS-adjacent, not $2 clicks.
- **Do not confuse contractor CPCs with your CPCs.** LocaliQ / WordStream home-services figures (HVAC ~$5–$24, plumbing ~$6–$21, roofing much higher) are what *your customers* pay to get homeowners. Irrelevant to bidding on “contractor website Concord.”

**Local Services Ads:** Google’s US eligibility list is trades, legal, health, beauty, auto, dining, etc. **Website design and custom software are not on the list.** Official: https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841. SearchLight Feb 2026 LSA home-services CPL **$53** blended is interesting for *clients*, not for Güd Vector as an advertiser.

**Sourced reason to skip:** A $1,000 month at $6–$14 CPC is ~70–160 clicks. At a 3–5% landing conversion (B2B SaaS / business-services band), that is a handful of form-fills, many of them “how much for a logo” or student-agency shoppers. One custom-website + AI job might still pay for the test — but the Concord finder is a cheaper way to get the same conversation. Revisit Ads only with (a) industry landing pages live, (b) exact-match on `[website for plumber Concord]` / `[Jobber alternative small shop]`, (c) a hard $500 cap and call tracking.

**Sources:** WordStream 2026 Google Ads Benchmarks https://www.wordstream.com/blog/2026-google-ads-benchmarks; WordStream “How Much Does Google Ads Cost?”; PipeRocket B2B SaaS Aug 2026; Google LSA eligibility; SearchLight LSA Feb 2026.

### 10. Local Facebook groups, Nextdoor Business, Craigslist — **be honest: mostly junk for this seller**

**Facebook groups**

- **Homeowner neighborhood groups** (“who’s a good plumber in Concord?”) are where *contractors* get jobs. PipelineOn cites Facebook community research that **67%** of group members have hired a provider recommended in-group. That is **your customer’s** channel, not yours. Posting “I build websites for plumbers” in a Concord parents group is spam.
- **Contractor / trade Facebook groups** (Bay Area plumbers, landscapers, “Contractor Talk” style): possible if you **answer** “what are you using instead of Jobber?” with a short, non-pitchy take and a link only when asked. Practitioner note (PipelineOn / FeedbackWrench pattern): helpful posts get tagged ~3× more than pitches. Volume **unknown**. Reputation risk if it reads like an agency dump.
- **Web-design Facebook groups:** filled with other designers and, separately, a well-documented 2025–2026 fake-client scam pattern (mailed-check / “SUPER FLEXIBLE” restaurant briefs). Not a buyer channel.

**Nextdoor**

- Audience is **verified homeowners**. Nextdoor’s own positioning and 2026 contractor-ad guides (Blue Grid, Valley Marketing Group): 1 in 3 US households active; **77% homeowners**. Ad CPC commonly **$2.50–$5**; test budgets **$150–$500/mo**; Local Deals from a few dollars per neighborhood.
- **Wrong buyer.** A Concord homeowner does not hire Güd Vector. A Concord landscaper might *have* a Nextdoor Business Page — claiming Güd Vector’s own page is fine ($0), advertising on Nextdoor is not.

**Craigslist**

- SF Bay **services** posts are often a **~$5** platform fee (pass-through cited by posting services in 2026). Ads expire ~30 days.
- **Reputation:** this is where homeowners hunt cheap labor and where web-design scams cluster. A “I will build your contractor website” post sits next to $99 logo ads. It trains the wrong price expectation and burns the brand in the one metro you live in.
- **Verdict: skip.** If you ever test, one post, one city, no claims you cannot put on gudvector.com, and treat replies as hostile until proven local.

**Sources:** PipelineOn Facebook groups for contractors; Blue Grid Nextdoor Ads 2026; Valley Marketing Group Nextdoor 2026; LiveAdPosting Craigslist services $5 fee; FTC 2026 social-ad scam alert; documented web-design client-scam writeups 2025.
