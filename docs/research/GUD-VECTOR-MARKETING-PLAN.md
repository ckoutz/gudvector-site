# Güd Vector marketing plan

**For:** Güd Vector Consulting Services (gudvector.com)  
**Researched:** 2 Sep 2026  
**Constraint:** Grounded claims only. No invented stats. Site copy today does not say “AI,” does not name Jobber, and has no proof — recommendations below respect that.

This plan is built from a live site audit of gudvector.com, buyer research (SBA, Jobber, Invoca, BrightLocal, G2, LocaliQ, RapidWebLaunch), a competitor map of Bay Area agencies plus Jobber / Housecall Pro / ServiceTitan / QuoteIQ, and a channel ranking for an owner-operator budget.

---

## 1. Audience and buying behavior

**Who buys.** The owner. Phase 5 (2021, 2,000+ North American SMBs) found the owner has final software say 80% of the time. SBA Advocacy (Feb 2026, Census 2022) reports 82.3% of U.S. small firms have no payroll staff, so an office manager often does not exist. Construction is even more solo: CPWR/Census NES 2023, 78.2% of construction establishments are nonemployers. Spouse and bookkeeper show up as cash-flow influencers, not measured co-signers. Jobber’s own 2026 survey was of 1,050 *owners*. Sell to the person on the truck or at the kitchen table, in their language: jobs, cash, looking professional, not becoming the software admin.

**How big.** Copy Jobber/Housecall Pro size, not ServiceTitan. Enlyft: both Jobber and Housecall Pro customers are most often 1–10 employees and $0–1M revenue (97% / 96% under 50 staff). Jobber (May 2026): 100k businesses, 400k pros. Housecall Pro: “200,000+ Pros,” Basic $59/mo, no long-term contract. ServiceTitan FY2026: ~10,800 customers, $82.1B GTV — about $7.6M invoiced per customer [derived]. That is a different market. Titan is widely quoted as “not optimized” for 3 or fewer techs. Güd Vector’s buyer is the 1–15 person shop that has already felt a template’s limits, not a PE roll-up.

**What triggers a look.** Lost jobs, not “custom AI.”

- **Missed calls.** Invoca (Jul 2026, 70M+ home-services calls): only 52% of callers speak with a person. 38% of answered calls are leads; 45% of those convert on the call. 55% of shops never ask the lead to buy on the call.
- **Slow follow-up.** Jobber (Dec 2025 / pub 2026, n=1,050): over 55% of customers want a reply within the hour; HVAC shops hit that 11–17% of the time. Cleaning is fastest at 26%.
- **Expensive leads.** LocaliQ 2025 (3,211 U.S. home-services search campaigns): blended CPL $90.92; plumbing/HVAC ~$128–$129; roofing $228.15. A missed call is a burned ad dollar.
- **Weak or missing site.** RapidWebLaunch 2026 (131,514 Google-listed businesses): 17% have no website; electricians only 71.7% have one; San Francisco 82.7% — not a digital vanguard. Clutch 2025 (n=406 owners): 17% have no site; of those, 34% say “not relevant to my industry,” only 16% cite cost. BrightLocal 2026 (n=1,002): after good reviews, 54% hit the website next (up from 32% in 2019). A strong Angi/Yelp page plus a missing domain leaks the exact moment the homeowner tries to confirm them.
- **Quotes that die in silence.** Jobber: quoting is a top-three time drain (37%). Level’s contractor-finance dataset (vendor, not census): tens of thousands of quotes created but never sent; converted quotes close in a median 2 days, lost ones linger 29. Mechanism matches owner-approved quoting plus a portal.

Do **not** use “62% unanswered,” “85% never call back,” or “78% hire the first responder.” Those lines are untraceable or mis-pinned on HBR 2011.

**Where they look.** For *hiring a contractor*: Google still #1, but BrightLocal 2026 has ChatGPT/genAI for local recs jumping 6% → 45% (now 3rd). For *hiring software*: G2 Mar 2026 (n=1,076 B2B buyers) — 51% start in an AI chatbot more often than Google (was 29% in Apr 2025); 33% bought a vendor they had never heard of; review-site citations are the #1 trust signal inside an AI answer (45%). For *hiring a vendor like Güd Vector*: they ask another owner, a bookkeeper, or an insurance agent. Jobber 2026: 59% of shops say referrals and repeat work are their top lead sources. They are not sitting on LinkedIn hunting for a web agency.

**Objections to custom.** Sticker shock vs Jobber Core $29–$49/mo or Housecall Basic $59/mo. Time vs a 14-day trial. Lock-in (“what if the shop disappears”). “I’m not a tech person” (Clutch: 15% of no-website owners cite lack of skills). Fear of becoming the admin — the ServiceTitan lesson the market already learned. SaaS already bundles a “good enough” website (Jobber includes a builder on every plan; Housecall sells Websites as an add-on). Custom cannot win on “faster than Jobber.” It wins on: the SaaS made you the admin, and it still does not match how you quote, get paid, pause, or cancel.

**Bay Area lever.** BLS May 2025 via EC&M: SF–Oakland–Fremont electrician mean $49.85/hr. Labor is expensive here, so “quotes that go out tonight” and “never miss the after-hours call” price easier than in a $25-wage market. Work PHCC East Bay / SF–San Mateo and ABC NorCal (~500 firms), not CEA’s $30B general contractors.

**Sources:** [Invoca 2026](https://www.invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026), [Jobber 2026 trends](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/), [LocaliQ 2025](https://localiq.com/blog/home-services-search-advertising-benchmarks/), [RapidWebLaunch 2026](https://rapidweblaunch.com/state-of-small-business-web-design), [BrightLocal 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), [G2 Mar 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-g2-research-half-of-b2b-software-buyers-now-start-their-research-with-ai-chatbots-302742807.html), [SBA Feb 2026](https://advocacy.sba.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FINAL_FAQsAboutSmallBusiness_2026_012826.pdf), [Clutch 2025](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025), [ServiceTitan FY2026](https://investors.servicetitan.com/news-releases/news-release-details/servicetitan-announces-fiscal-fourth-quarter-and-full-fiscal).

## 2. Competitive landscape

Güd Vector sits in a hole most vendors refuse to occupy: a **site the shop owns**, plus a **system built around that shop’s quote rules**, with the **owner still in the loop**. Local web agencies sell a site. HighLevel shops sell a $197–$397/mo template. Field-service SaaS sells a login. AI receptionists answer phones. Almost nobody ships quote-approval + pay/pause/cancel portal + scheduling as one system under a custom site.

### Direct (custom / done-for-you)

| Who | What they actually sell | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| **Tenaya360** (Walnut Creek) | Contractor WordPress $4.5k–$12k, care from $249/mo, SEO $799–$1,399/mo. Named local clients (Brovelli, MFC Electric, RGC Construction). | Closest local peer. Stays as a marketing partner. Public packages stop at forms, galleries, local-search pages. |
| **Sama Nova** (Bay Area only) | $1,500 / $2,800 / $4,500+/mo retainers. Site + ads + CRM capture + text follow-up. | Marketing system on a retainer. AI is lead follow-up, not owner-gated quoting. |
| **Primate Web FX / Contractor Site Design / Webpage Conversion / Home Service Direct** | National contractor-web factories. $999 in 5 days up to $15k. | Site in weeks. Quoting/portal/scheduling = “embed Jobber” at best. |
| **Novaica / LeadWise / Royalso** | HighLevel snapshots. Novaica $297/mo, $0 setup, live in 14 days. Cancel = lose the site. | Looks done-for-you. Shared platform, generic AI estimates, no owner gate. |
| **DK Studio** | Custom agents $15k–$60k. Claude/GPT/LangGraph. Not trade-specific. | Custom-AI peer on price, zero home-service distribution. |

No named Bay Area firm publicly sells *custom field-service software* as a productized offer to trades. That absence is the opening.

### Indirect (self-serve)

**Status quo** is the real competitor for Concord landscapers and cleaners: a Hotmail address, Angi/Yelp, a notebook, quotes in text threads. The existing finder already shows ~1 in 5 checked shops in Concord landscaping/cleaning still have no site or a clearly weak one.

### Six gaps to exploit (each tied to a sourced weakness)

1. **Owner-gated quoting vs template/self-serve.** Jobber has no native customer self-quoting. QuoteIQ’s InstaQuote is the homeowner pricing themselves. EstimatorSuite 2026 (n=287): 47% still quote in sheets/paper; under-24-hour bids win 31% more often. *If* Güd Vector actually drafts quotes with a model and the owner approves before they go out, say that. The live site currently says “You send the quote.” Do not advertise AI drafting until that sentence is true.
2. **A website they own vs a CRM subdomain.** Kore Komfort (Mar 2026) and Untap Web: Jobber/Housecall builders have no blog, no city pages, no serious schema; cancel the CRM and the site dies. Novaica FAQ: cancel and you lose the website.
3. **One business’s rules, not a one-size pricebook.** ServiceTitan is per-tech and months to live. JobNimbus caps Essentials at 10 automations / 2 integrations. Pause policy, trip fee, “we don’t quote that over the phone” as code, not a checkbox.
4. **Pay / pause / cancel portal vs an invoice pay link.** Jobber Client Hub = approve / see appointment / pay. Square/Wix/Calendly cancel a 30-minute slot, not a project or seasonal contract. Güd Vector’s portal (live at `/portal`, noindex) already does accept/reject, pay, pause/cancel. That is the proof, even without a case study.
5. **Done-for-you encoding vs a 14-day trial they abandon.** EstimatorSuite: 28% of non-adopters cite learning curve, 22% too many tools. Smith.ai/Ruby answer phones; they don’t draft quotes. DK Studio’s generic agents are $15k–$60k with no trade website.
6. **Bay Area shop vs remote template mill.** Tenaya360 and Sama Nova win on local presence. Neither ships the product stack. Güd Vector can be the local custom-system shop they are not.

**Sources:** vendor pricing pages fetched 2 Sep 2026 ([Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/), [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/)), [Tenaya360](https://tenaya360.com/), [Sama Nova](https://samanova.io/), [Novaica](https://novaica.com/), plus the competitor brief in `03-competitors.md`.

## 3. Positioning and messaging

**Current site, in its own words.** Title: “Websites & Systems for Bay Area Service Businesses.” H1: “If they can't find you, they call someone else.” Motto: “sending your company in the right direction.” Two pillars: website building, and “Automating business systems” (quote → portal to review, set up service, pay, pause/cancel). Who it’s for: “One site, one system, less to manage.” The word “AI” does not appear. No testimonials, logos, case studies, prices, phone, or street. Three indexable URLs.

The H1 is already the right consumer-side pain (BrightLocal 54% hit the site after reviews). The gap is the **buyer-side** pain: “I will not become the software admin.” And the **proof** gap: a partner who Googles you finds a homepage and legal pages.

**Positioning (one sentence).**  
Güd Vector builds a custom website and a custom system around how a Bay Area service shop already works — the owner approves every quote, the customer pays/pauses/cancels in a branded portal, and the site is theirs. Not a template they have to adapt to, and not another dashboard they have to learn.

**Why this, specifically:**
- *Owner stays in the loop* because QuoteIQ’s pitch is the opposite (InstaQuote) and Jobber’s AI/receptionist path is “book while you’re on the roof.” Jobber 2026: quoting is a top time drain, and 37% of owners have no plans to adopt AI. Control is the feature.
- *Site they own* because Jobber/Housecall/Novaica sites die or stay thin when the subscription dies (Kore Komfort, Untap Web, Novaica FAQ).
- *Built around how you work* because ServiceTitan’s 12–16 week setup and JobNimbus’s automation caps are the market teaching shops that “one-size” means “you become the admin.”
- *One site, one system* is already on the homepage and matches the brief. Keep it. Do not replace it with “AI platform.”

**What not to say yet.** Do not lead with “bespoke AI” on the public site until quote-drafting is a real, owner-visible step (the live copy is still “You send the quote”). Do not name Jobber/Housecall on the homepage; name them on comparison pages where a fair table can live. Do not invent testimonials, years in business, or “we replace ServiceTitan.” Never use the word “ops.” Keep “sending,” not “putting,” in motto variants.

**Headline / angle ideas (each tied to a reason):**

1. **“If they can’t find you, they call someone else.”** Keep. It’s true (BrightLocal 54%) and already indexed.
2. **“You approve the quote. Then it goes out.”** Because QuoteIQ lets the homeowner price the job, and Jobber does not natively self-quote — owner-gated is the hole.
3. **“The site is yours. Canceling us doesn’t kill it.”** Because Novaica/Jobber/Housecall website lock-in is a sourced complaint.
4. **“One site, one system, less to manage.”** Keep. Jobber 2026 time drains are jobsite, customer communication, and quoting — not “need more software.”
5. **“We encode how you already work. You don’t learn a product.”** Because 28% of EstimatorSuite non-adopters cited learning curve, and ServiceTitan is the cautionary tale.
6. **“Not Jobber. Not a $297/mo template. A system for *this* shop.”** For comparison pages only, with a fair “when Jobber is the right answer” row so LLMs will cite you.

**Proof points that would actually move this buyer (in order of honesty):**
- A live portal they can tap (you already have `/portal` — show a client-branded preview, not Güd Vector’s own checkout as the hero).
- One named East Bay shop, even if the site is a draft (AB Gardening / Lopez-style previews are the start; a live URL is the close).
- Side-by-side: their current Angi/Yelp vs a mobile page with click-to-call and real services (no invented services).
- A 36-month TCO card vs Jobber Grow/Housecall MAX + seats + processing + add-ons — Phase 5: 81% say cost is influential; 37% rated vendor cost transparency poor-to-adequate.
- Google reviews for Güd Vector itself (BrightLocal: 47% won’t use a business with fewer than 20 reviews). Partners behave like cautious consumers when they stake their name.

**Voice.** Owner, not engineer. Jobs, cash, phone, quotes. Short sentences. No architecture diagrams. One screen: approve / don’t approve. Email is currently the only public contact — that’s honest; add a phone or bookable call once you can answer it, because this buyer loses leads to missed calls and will judge you by the same standard.

## 5. Content / SEO opportunities

gudvector.com currently has **three indexable URLs** (`/`, `/privacy`, `/terms`). About/Services/Contact are homepage anchors. `/blog`, `/industries`, `/pricing`, `/work` 404. Privacy and terms wrongly canonicalize to the homepage. Schema is `ProfessionalService` with no phone, no street, no `sameAs`, no FAQ. No analytics detected. The site sells “local SEO” for clients and does not demonstrate it for itself.

Every new page should be extractable by Google **and** by ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini: named entity, answer in the first 40–60 words, a table, FAQPage JSON-LD, dated facts, links to official Jobber/Housecall/ServiceTitan pages, Bing as well as Google. Neutral comparison voice — Perplexity down-ranks self-congratulation.

## 6. Quick wins vs. long-term plays

### This week (cash ≈ $0)

- Claim/complete Google Business Profile. Same entity string as the site. Photos of real drafts, not stock.
- Ask every past/current customer for a Google review. No paid-for-positive (FTC + Google).
- Work the **12 Concord qualified names** by phone + one personalized mobile preview. Two-week calling block, not a sequence. Do not batch-mail them.
- Fix privacy/terms canonical + OG URLs so they do not collapse into the homepage.

### This quarter (under ~$1,000 cash, 5–8 hours/week)

- Coffee with 3 bookkeepers and 2 insurance producers. One-pager. One sentence they can say.
- Guest the next PHCC ACCC dinner **or** CLCA SF Bay Area meeting. Join **one** associate seat (CLCA $555/yr if landscaping stays the wedge; PHCC $195/quarter if plumbers/HVAC are next).
- Ship pages 1, 5, 10, and 12 first (landscaper geo, owner-approved quoting, “don’t want to learn software,” Concord hub). Then one comparison (Jobber).
- One named proof: a live client URL, or an honest “preview we made for [shop]” with permission. Partners will Google you.
- Free Clutch profile.

### Do not spend this quarter

Google Ads, LSA, Clutch paid, Houzz, Angi, Craigslist, Nextdoor ads, Sales Navigator, BNI dues, a general blog, joining four associations at once.

### Long-term (sustained investment)

- Remaining industry and comparison pages; one YouTube/article pair per comparison (contractors actually watch “Jobber vs Housecall”). LinkedIn is for CPAs, not C-27 owners.
- Expand the finder by geo (Antioch, Oakland hills) or trade (inspectors, environmental testing, plumbers) after Concord landscaping/cleaning is worked. Finite lists; do not “scale a sequence.”
- Review engine to 20+ Google reviews (BrightLocal threshold).
- Revisit search ads only with industry landings live, exact-match on `[website for plumber Concord]` / `[Jobber alternative small shop]`, a hard $500 cap, and call tracking.
- Productize a **fixed scope and a go-live date** (site + quote-approve + portal + schedule). Time-to-value is SaaS’s honest advantage. A vague “custom project, months” loses to a 14-day trial even when the product is better.
- Write the contract so they **own the site, the data, and the pause/cancel rules.** Custom lock-in fear is the mirror of Titan lock-in.
