# Güd Vector audience brief: who buys websites and field-service systems

**Purpose.** Evidence for a marketing plan. Güd Vector is a Bay Area shop that builds a **custom website plus a custom AI system** (quotes with owner approval, customer portal for pay/pause/cancel, scheduling) — not self-serve SaaS.

**How to read this.** Every numbered claim has a source URL and a paraphrase of what that source actually said. Years are labeled. Derived math from official figures is marked **[derived]**. Secondary roundups are labeled as such. Stats that circulate without a recoverable primary (especially “62% of calls unanswered” and “85% never call back”) are flagged, not treated as fact.

**Audience in one line.** Owner-operators and small crews in home services, trades, inspection, environmental testing, and field service — typically 1–15 people, often under $1–2M revenue — who still take the software and website decision themselves, with an office manager, bookkeeper, or spouse as influencer, not as the signer.

## 1. Who decides, and how big is the buyer?

### 1.1 The owner is the economic buyer

1. **Owner has final say ~80% of the time (2021, North America SMBs).** Phase 5 + Cargo surveyed 2,000+ SMBs. “The small business owner himself/herself wields influence over final purchase decisions 80% of the time.” Internal end-users were “influential or very influential” for 69% of owners (72% at 50+ employees). Cost was “influential or very influential” for 81%. Online reviews ranked second as a helpful influence.  
   Source: https://www.phase-5.com/news-insights/insights/new-research-smb-software-purchase-decision-drivers-phase-5  
   *Year: 2021 (pandemic-era; still the cleanest quantified owner-vs-staff split found).*

2. **Among the smallest firms, the buyer is almost always a sole decision-maker (2024).** SMB Group’s 2024 SMB Technology Buying Journey Survey (738 U.S. SMB finance/tech decision-makers, June 2024) reported 69% sole decision-maker overall. In the 1-employee band the figure was 98% sole; 2 employees 60%; 3–4 employees 70%; 5–9 employees 60%; 10–19 employees 62%; 20–49 employees 64%. Joint decision-making rises with headcount.  
   Source (PDF): https://www.smb-gr.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/2024-SMB-Buying-Journey-Final.pdf  
   *Note: the PDF host returned 500 when re-fetched; the 69% / size-band figures were extracted from an earlier successful retrieval of that same document. Treat as 2024 primary pending a live re-download.*

3. **Most U.S. small firms have no payroll staff at all — so “office manager” often does not exist.** SBA Office of Advocacy (Feb 2026 FAQ, using Census 2022 data): 36.2 million small businesses; **82.3% (29.8 million) are nonemployer firms** (no paid employees); 17.7% are employer firms. About **26.1% of employer firms were family-owned** in 2022. Construction is one of the largest small-business sectors: SBA’s 2025 U.S. profile lists **2,875,590 construction nonemployers** and **718,831 construction employer firms**.  
   Sources:  
   - https://advocacy.sba.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FINAL_FAQsAboutSmallBusiness_2026_012826.pdf  
   - https://advocacy.sba.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/United_States_2025-State-Profile.pdf  
   - Census NES 2022: https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2025/05/smallest-businesses.html — Construction was 9.6% of 2022 nonemployer establishments (2,875,590 firms) with $238.0 billion in receipts.

4. **Construction specifically is a solo-heavy industry.** CPWR (Census NES): in 2023, **78.2% of construction establishments were nonemployer**.  
   Source: https://www.cpwr.com/research/data-center/the-construction-chart-book/interactive-7th/industry-businesses/nonemployer/

5. **Office manager / bookkeeper / spouse: influencers, not well-measured co-buyers.** No 2024–2026 primary survey was found that splits field-service *software* purchase authority among owner vs office manager vs spouse vs bookkeeper. Closest evidence:
   - Phase 5 (2021): owner 80%; end-users 69% influential; cost 81% influential — which is the bookkeeper/accountant lane.
   - SMB sales literature consistently describes a cash-flow gatekeeper (owner, accountant, bookkeeper, or office manager who tracks recurring charges) after the owner is sold. This is **not a measured share**.  
     Example (secondary): https://www.elephantedge.ai/blog/selling-saas-to-us-who-make-decision-in-us-small-businesses/
   - “Spouse” appears constantly in *homeowner* buying research (both spouses at the estimate), **not** in contractor-software buying research. Do not treat “I need to talk to my spouse” as a documented software-buying role. In owner-operator households the spouse often runs books or answers the phone; that is ethnography, not a stat.

6. **Jobber’s own 2026 owner survey is owner-centric by design.** Jobber surveyed **1,050 U.S. home service business owners** (Conjointly, Dec 2025, ±3 pts at 90% confidence) across cleaning, HVAC, roofing, plumbing, electrical, lawn/tree care, and general contracting — “a mix of solo operators, growing teams, and established businesses.” They segment by revenue, maturity, and owner age, not by office-manager title. That is itself a signal of who they believe buys.  
   Source: https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/

### What this means for Güd Vector

Sell to the **owner**, in the owner’s language (jobs, cash, looking professional, not being the software admin). Copy the Jobber/HCP *size* (1–15 people, often under $2M), not ServiceTitan’s. Do not wait for an office manager who frequently does not exist. Bring a one-page cost comparison a bookkeeper can defend. Treat “custom” as the path for shops that have already felt SaaS limits — unique quoting rules, inspection/environmental workflows, wanting the website and the job system to be one thing — not as a first tool for a brand-new solo.

## 2. Purchase triggers

### 2.1 Missed calls and slow lead response

7. **Invoca 2026 (strongest current primary on phone leads):** anonymized Invoca platform data across carpeting, construction, doors & windows, home security, HVAC, lawn & trees, pest control, plumbing, and restoration, inside a dataset of **70M+ calls**.  
   - **52% of callers to home services speak with a person** (all inbound).  
   - **65%** answered on calls lasting >15 seconds; **73%** on calls >30 seconds.  
   - Answer rates by sub-industry range **32%–74%**.  
   - **38%** of person-answered calls are leads; **45%** of those leads convert on the call.  
   - **55% of home services businesses don’t ask the lead to buy or book on the call.**  
   - ChatGPT-referred calls (first year with enough volume): **45% lead rate** (highest of any channel) but **41% convert**; volume still “very low.”  
   - Lifting answer rate, lead rate, and conversion **5 points each ≈ 38% more conversions from the same call volume.**  
   Source: https://www.invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026  
   *Published July 2026. Invoca’s customers are typically advertisers using call tracking — likely more sophisticated than a typical 3-person shop. Directional, not a census of all contractors.*

8. **Older Invoca “27% unanswered” (2023/2024) is widely repeated; the 2026 report supersedes it.** Secondary pages (Hicira, Made For Builders, MostAI Labs) cite Invoca ~2024: **27% of inbound home-services calls unanswered**, and ~**$1,200 lost per missed sales call** (HVAC replacements >$3,500). The original Invoca 2024 PDF was not recovered in this pass. Prefer the 2026 52%-answered figure.  
   Secondary examples: https://www.hicira.com/missed-call-statistics — https://mostailabs.com/ai-for/home-services

9. **Do not use “62% of small-business calls go unanswered” as a home-services fact.** MostAI Labs traces it to a **411 Locals observation of 85 businesses, roughly a decade old**, and says the companion “85% of missed callers never call back” has **no traceable primary**.  
   Source: https://mostailabs.com/ai-for/home-services

10. **Jobber 2026, from the owner’s side (n=1,050, Dec 2025):**  
    - **Over 55% of customers expect a response within the hour; 28% expect an immediate reply.**  
    - **60% of pros reply to leads the same day; 20% within the hour.**  
    - HVAC is slowest: **just 11%** (elsewhere in the same report: 17%) respond within the hour. Cleaning is fastest: **26% within the hour.**  
    - Established businesses: **87% respond within a day.**  
    - **1 in 4 customers now cite response speed as a key hiring factor.**  
    Source: https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/

11. **Lead-response research is old but still the citation everyone uses (2011).** Harvard Business Review, March 2011, “The Short Life of Online Sales Leads” (Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington). The article paywall blocked a full-text scrape; the widely quoted findings from that study / the related InsideSales work are: contact within **5 minutes vs 30 minutes → ~21× more likely to qualify**; within an hour vs later → ~7×; many firms took **~42 hours** to respond. **Year: 2011. Not home-services-specific.**  
    Source: https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads  
    *The “78% hire the first contractor who responds” line is routinely mis-attributed to HBR. A recoverable HBR sentence matching 78% was **not** found. Treat 78% as folklore unless a primary is produced.*

12. **Paid leads are expensive, so a missed call is a burned ad dollar (LocaliQ 2025).** 3,211 U.S. home-services search campaigns, Apr 1 2024–Mar 21 2025 (medians):  
    - Blended CPL **$90.92** (up for 69% of advertisers, +10.51% YoY).  
    - Plumbing **$129.02**; Heating & furnaces **$129.02**; AC install/repair **$127.74**; Electricians **$93.69**; Roofing & gutters **$228.15**; General contractors **$165.67**; Cleaning **$46.99**.  
    - Blended conversion rate **7.33%** (down 14.96% YoY); general contractors **2.61%**; roofing **3.70%**; plumbing **7.63%**.  
    - Blended CPC **$7.85**.  
    Source: https://localiq.com/blog/home-services-search-advertising-benchmarks/

### 2.2 Bad / no website, looking less professional than competitors

13. **Clutch 2025 owner survey (n=406 U.S. SBOs, Aug 5 2025):** **83% have a website** (up from 64% in 2018); **17% do not**; **12% launched in the past year**. Of those without a site: **34% say it’s “not relevant to my industry”**; only **16% cite cost** (down from 26% in 2018). **84% of the no-website group cite reasons other than cost.** Referrals are the top lead source for **39%** of businesses without a website. Of those *with* a site, **40%** say SEO is their top lead source. **90% plan to invest in the website in the next 12 months** (58% full redesign, 53% speed, 45% mobile, 37% SEO). **12% used custom/full-code development.**  
    Source: https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025  
    *Survey of owners who answered an online poll — likely more digital than a random plumber.*

14. **RapidWebLaunch 2026 census of Google-listed local businesses (stronger for trades):** census of 423,009 Google-listed businesses in 24 cities; random sample **131,514**; **91,904 websites audited**. Updated Sep 2, 2026.  
    - **17% of Google-listed local businesses have no website** (83% list one). Of listed sites: **1.1%** are just Facebook/a directory; **8.2%** did not load.  
    - **Electricians 71.7%** have a website (n=3,034) — one of the weakest trades. Painters 78.4%; cleaning 77.7%; plumbers 80.4%; HVAC 83.9%; landscapers 82.6%; roofers 81.4%.  
    - **Only 28% put a contact form on the homepage.** **42% have no analytics.** Average mobile Lighthouse **58/100**. **22% fail Google’s basic mobile checks.**  
    - Independents trail chains on HTTPS, blogs, analytics, schema.  
    - **San Francisco: 82.7% have a website**, score 72/100, 6,320 businesses sampled — middle of the pack, not a digital vanguard.  
    Source: https://rapidweblaunch.com/state-of-small-business-web-design

15. **BrightLocal 2026 consumer survey (n=1,002 U.S. adults) on the website as a trust step after reviews:**  
    - **97% read reviews for local businesses**; **41% “always”** (up from 29% in 2025).  
    - After positive reviews, **54% then check the business’s website** (up from 32% when last asked in 2019). **66% do more research vs 34% ready to buy.**  
    - BrightLocal states **“just 40% of local businesses having a dedicated website”** in that funnel discussion — a stricter cut than RapidWebLaunch’s 83% of *Google-listed* firms. The two numbers are not the same universe.  
    - **31% will only use a business with 4.5+ stars** (up from 17% in 2025); **68% require 4+ stars**. **47% won’t use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.** ChatGPT/generative AI for local recommendations: **6% → 45% year over year**, third-most-used source after Google and Facebook.  
    Source: https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/

16. **Paid-search conversion as a proxy for “website is leaking money.”** LocaliQ 2025: home-services search-ad conversion **7.33%** median; general contracting **2.61%**. That is ad-landing conversion, not organic homepage conversion. Agency blogs often quote “median contractor site 2–4%” without publishing their sample; **do not treat 2–4% as a primary**. The LocaliQ 2.61%–7.33% band is the solid number.

### 2.3 Quote follow-up failure

17. **Jobber 2026:** 69% of pros report a **win rate over 50%**; more than a third close **over 70%**. Starting businesses: **21% report win rates under 30%**, and many don’t track. Jobber platform aside: businesses offering optional line items see **25–50% upsell rates**, but **only 16% of pros offer good/better/best**. Quoting is one of the top three time drains (**37%**), after jobsite management (48%) and customer communication (40%).  
    Source: https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/

18. **Vendor/advisor studies on unsent and unfollowed quotes (useful, not census):**  
    - Level (contractor finance blog, citing an FSM quote dataset): **438,000 quotes, 183,000 converted (42% raw)**; among quotes that actually reached a customer and got a decision, median **73.9%**. **48,000 quotes created but never sent.** Converted quotes close in a **median 2 days**; lost quotes linger **29 days**.  
      https://levelcfo.com/blog/quote-conversion-rate-contractors/  
    - Same author’s 7-day rule: median contractor converts **38% of quotes sent**.  
      https://levelcfo.com/blog/quote-follow-up-7-day-rule/  
    - These are **not independently audited**. They support the *mechanism* (quotes die in silence) that Güd Vector’s owner-approved quote + auto-follow-up is built to fix.

### 2.4 SaaS switching after painful onboarding / looking more professional

19. **ServiceTitan implementation is the cautionary tale the small shop already knows.** Secondary contractor guides (not Titan’s site) put go-live at **30–90 days**, often **3–6 months**, implementation **$5k–$50k+**, 12-month contracts, early-termination fees. Review themes: “paying for months before going live,” complexity, dedicated admin required.  
    Examples: https://pineido.com/blog/servicetitan-vs-housecall-pro-vs-jobber/ — https://contractorguidepro.com/servicetitan-vs-jobber-contractors/

20. **Jobber/HCP sell “days not months.”** Jobber: 14-day full Grow trial, no card; self-serve. HCP: 14-day MAX trial, no card, “no long-term contracts.” That *is* the alternative the market already offers. Güd Vector cannot win on “faster than Jobber.” It can win on **“the SaaS made you the admin, and it still doesn’t match how you quote / how customers pay, pause, or cancel.”**

21. **Jobber 2026 on professionalism as a hiring factor:** workmanship 69%, **clear communication 32%**, **professional behavior 31%**. Price 62% and reviews 51% still dominate *choice*; **14% value online booking** (higher among younger/high-confidence operators). Top Jobber customers: **Google 4.5+ and 50+ reviews**.  
    Source: https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/

22. **AI is now a competitive trigger, not a novelty.** Jobber 2026: **52% of owners already use AI** day-to-day (quoting 54%, invoicing 52%, writing 51%); **88% of “high-confidence” (fully booked, growing) businesses use AI vs 27% of low-confidence**. HVAC AI adoption **81.5%** — highest of any trade. **64% of owners under 30** already use AI. **37% have no plans to adopt**; nearly 1 in 5 don’t believe AI will help.  
    Source: https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/  
    Jobber community recap of the same survey: https://community.getjobber.com/discussions/operations-forum/ai-for-contractors-how-home-service-pros-are-using-ai-in-2026/9398

### What this means for Güd Vector

Lead with **lost jobs**, not “we build custom AI.” The evidence stack that matches Güd Vector’s product: (1) **half of home-services callers never reach a person** (Invoca 2026); (2) **customers expect a same-hour reply** and most HVAC shops miss that (Jobber); (3) **leads already cost $90–$228** (LocaliQ); (4) **electricians especially still skip a real website** (RapidWebLaunch 28% of electricians have none); (5) **after reviews, 54% hit the website next** (BrightLocal) — a weak site wastes the review; (6) quotes die when nobody follows up, which is exactly what owner-approved quoting + a customer portal is for. Pitch **against becoming ServiceTitan’s unpaid implementation lead**, not against having software.

## 3. Where they search and who they ask

### 3.1 How *homeowners* find *the contractor* (the pressure on the shop’s public face)

23. **BrightLocal 2026 (n=1,002):** Google still #1 for local recommendations but share **83% → 71%**. Facebook, BBB, Angi, Yelp, Apple Maps all **up**. **ChatGPT/other genAI: 6% → 45%**, now **3rd**. Average consumer uses **six review sites**. Top sites for *writing* reviews: Google 45%, Facebook 34%, **Yelp 24%**, Apple Maps 17%, Tripadvisor 16%, **BBB 16%**.  
    Source: https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/

24. **Jobber 2026 on how shops get work:** **59% say referrals and repeat work are their top lead sources.** High-confidence businesses add **Google (20%), Facebook (32%), Local Services Ads (19%)**. Younger owners: Instagram, YouTube, LSAs. Jobber’s own site-audit of customers: **43% don’t clearly list service areas**, **65% don’t show hours**, **74% missing an FAQ**.  
    Source: https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/

25. **Invoca 2026 on channels that produce *phone* leads:** paid search is still the volume engine; Google Business Profile plays that role on the organic side. Generative AI (ChatGPT) is the **highest-intent / lowest-volume** new channel.  
    Source: https://www.invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026

26. **LocaliQ:** Google sees ~**180,000 U.S. searches/month for “plumber near me”** and **~78,000 for “handyman.”** Yelp: home services led new business openings in every U.S. state in 2024 (LocaliQ citing Yelp). NYT via LocaliQ: renovation buyers now often request **six or more bids**.  
    Source: https://localiq.com/blog/home-services-search-advertising-benchmarks/

    Source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-g2-research-half-of-b2b-software-buyers-now-start-their-research-with-ai-chatbots-302742807.html

28. **Phase 5 2021 (SMBs):** advertising, online reviews, and tech forums all help 60% of younger SMBs; **online reviews ranked second** across all SMBs; advertising mattered more to larger SMBs (66% vs 36% under 10 employees).  
    Source: https://www.phase-5.com/news-insights/insights/new-research-smb-software-purchase-decision-drivers-phase-5

29. **Peer owners remain the default for trades, even when unmeasured.** Jobber runs a **community of “20,000+ home service pros”** (pricing page). HCP: “Community of 30,000+ home service Pros.” That is where switching stories (onboarding pain, fee creep) spread. Trade associations (section 5) and accountants/bookkeepers are the other trusted referrers; **no 2024–2026 survey quantified their share of software vendor introductions.**

30. **Nextdoor / Facebook local groups, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Yelp** show up as *homeowner* recommendation surfaces (BrightLocal), not as documented *software* discovery channels. For Güd Vector they still matter: a contractor hunting a web shop will also search those names, and AI tools scrape them.

31. **Software review sites (G2, Capterra, Software Advice) are the trust layer for AI answers** (G2 2026, 45%). A Bay Area custom shop that is invisible on G2/Capterra/Clutch is invisible to ChatGPT’s shortlist.

### What this means for Güd Vector

Own three discovery paths: **(1) Google + GBP + reviews** (still how homeowners judge the shop, which is the pain you sell); **(2) ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini answers** to prompts like “custom website and quoting system for a Bay Area HVAC company that’s tired of Housecall Pro”; **(3) owner-to-owner** — PHCC chapters, ABC NorCal, chambers, bookkeepers. Put named case studies, Clutch/G2 profiles, and FAQ-structured pages on the public web so AI assistants can cite you. Facebook/Nextdoor are for *social proof in the owner’s world*, not for running ads as the primary close.

## 4. Objections to custom vs SaaS

There is no 2025–2026 survey titled “why contractors reject custom web/AI shops.” The objections below are **reconstructed from (a) why they buy Jobber/HCP, (b) why they complain about those tools, (c) Clutch’s reasons for skipping a website, and (d) build-vs-buy literature.** Each is tied to a source.

### 4.1 Cost, monthly vs project fee

32. **SaaS looks cheap on the sticker.** Jobber Core **$29–$49/mo** annual; HCP Basic **$59/mo** annual. A five-figure custom project fails a gut check against that — unless you show **3-year TCO** including per-seat fees, processing, add-ons, and the owner’s admin hours.  
    Sources: https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/ — https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/

33. **The sticker is not the bill — and reviewers already resent that.** HCP Capterra/G2 complaint themes (2026 roundups): **add-on creep** (proposals, GPS, price book, Voice), extra users **$75–$100/mo**, processing 2.59%+. Jobber: extra users, processing 2.9%+$0.30, plus paid add-ons (Receptionist $29, Pipeline $49, Marketing Suite $99 on the pricing page). ServiceTitan: unpublished pricing + implementation + 12–36 month contracts.  
    HCP pricing page extras: https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/  
    Jobber add-on prices: https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/

34. **Phase 5 2021:** **81%** say financial considerations are influential/very influential. **37%** rated current vendor cost transparency poor-to-adequate; **38%** said the pricing model poorly-to-adequately met needs. Only **56%** feel they get good/very good value.  
    Source: https://www.phase-5.com/news-insights/insights/new-research-smb-software-purchase-decision-drivers-phase-5

35. **Clutch 2025:** cost is **no longer the main reason** shops skip a website (16%). **Relevance** (“not for my industry,” 34%) and **social media as a substitute** (21% in Network Solutions’ 2026 recap of the same Clutch-style reasons) dominate.  
    Clutch: https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025  
    Network Solutions recap (reasons table: 34% not necessary, 21% prefer social, 26% cost, 15% lack skills — table mixes Clutch with other citations): https://www.networksolutions.com/blog/small-business-website-statistics/

36. **Custom TCO ranges in agency literature (not a survey):** $30k–$250k build; 15–25%/year maintain; SaaS wins if true all-in is under ~$2k/month.  
    https://routiine.io/blog/custom-software-vs-saas — https://blog.digitalheroesco.com/build-vs-buy/field-service-management-software/

### 4.2 Time, lock-in, “I’m not a tech person,” fear of being the admin

37. **Time-to-value is SaaS’s strongest honest advantage.** Jobber/HCP: days, 14-day trials, no card. ServiceTitan: months. Custom: **months, not days** (build-vs-buy guides say 3–7 months to MVP). Güd Vector has to productize a **fixed scope and a go-live date**, or this objection wins.

38. **Lock-in is already a SaaS grievance.** Jobber: month-to-month *or* 1-year commitment; annual prepaid non-refundable. HCP: “no long-term contracts” is a selling point aimed at Titan. Titan: 12–36 months, auto-renew, early-exit fees in contractor reports. Custom lock-in fear is the mirror image: “what if the agency disappears / I don’t own the code / I become the only person who understands it.”  
    Jobber FAQ: https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/  
    HCP FAQ: https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/

39. **“I’m not a tech person” is documented as a website barrier, not a software-title barrier.** Clutch/Network Solutions: **15%** of no-website owners cite lack of technical skills. Clutch: 9% of sites were built by the owner; 41% used no-code *because* it promised no skills.  
    https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025

40. **Fear of being the admin is the ServiceTitan lesson the market already learned.** Titan works when someone can live in the platform. Contractor advisors state explicitly: without a dedicated office person, shops use ~20% of features and pay 100% of price. Jobber/HCP win by promising the owner will *not* need that person. A custom system that still requires the owner to be sysadmin **fails the same test**. Güd Vector’s offer has to include **owner-approval on quotes** (control without admin) and **someone else running the system**.

41. **Jobber 2026 time drains** — jobsite (48%), customer communication (40%), quoting (37%) — are the jobs a custom AI system is supposed to take. If the pitch sounds like “another dashboard to log into,” it collides with the thing they are trying to escape.  
    https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/

42. **SaaS already bundles a “good enough” website.** Jobber: “Launch a professional website in minutes” on Core. HCP: “Websites — Get a professional website built and managed by Housecall Pro” as an add-on. That is the **substitute good**. Custom has to beat “the website that came with my scheduler,” not “no website.”

43. **Switching pain is real and cuts both ways.** Phase 5 2021: **31%** of SMBs likely to switch a current software provider in 12 months; **66%** among 50+ employee SMBs. So they *do* switch — but they switch to another package unless the custom story is specific.  
    https://www.phase-5.com/news-insights/insights/new-research-smb-software-purchase-decision-drivers-phase-5

### What this means for Güd Vector

Price as **a project with a maintenance retainer**, shown next to 36 months of Jobber Grow/HCP MAX + seats + processing + add-ons + the owner’s nights. Promise **you are the admin**; the owner only **approves quotes**. Write the contract so they **own the site, the data, and the pause/cancel rules**. Scope a **90-day go-live** for a standard package (site + quote-approve + portal + schedule) or you lose to a 14-day SaaS trial. Do not fight “I’m not technical” with architecture diagrams; fight it with **one screen: approve / don’t approve**.

## 5. Bay Area / Northern California

### 5.1 Labor cost (why software that saves an hour is worth more here)

44. **San Francisco–Oakland–Fremont is the highest-paid U.S. metro for electricians (BLS OEWS May 2025).** EC&M, citing that BLS release: mean **$49.85/hour, $103,690/year**.  
    Source: https://www.ecmweb.com/construction/article/55398829/the-top-10-highest-paying-cities-for-electricians-2025  
    BLS news release (bot-blocked this fetch; URL for the primary): https://www.bls.gov/regions/west/news-release/occupationalemploymentandwages_sanfrancisco.htm  
    *BLS: all-occupation mean hourly wage in the metro $48.19 vs U.S. $33.54 (May 2025).*

45. **Plumbers / HVAC, same metro, BLS OEWS 2025 (via Hyring tabulations of BLS):**  
    - Plumbers, pipefitters, steamfitters (SOC 47-2152): **median $77,880**; 25th–75th $62,650–$121,770; mean annual ~$92,380.  
      https://hyring.com/jobseeker-toolkit/salary/plumber-salary-in-san-francisco  
    - HVAC mechanics/installers (SOC 49-9021): **median $78,490**; 25th–75th $61,930–$101,870; mean ~$86,540.  
      https://hyring.com/jobseeker-toolkit/salary/hvac-technician-salary-in-san-francisco  
    *Prefer the BLS OEWS tables if a human re-pulls them; Hyring is a derivative of BLS, not an original survey.*

46. **Jobber 2026 national owners:** **50% raised prices because of labor costs**; 72% cited inflation. That pressure is sharper in the Bay Area given the wage premium above.  
    https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/

47. **SBA size standards (context, not local):** plumbing/HVAC/electrical contractors (NAICS 238210 / 238220) are “small” up to **$19M** average annual receipts. Güd Vector’s buyers sit far below that line.  
    https://www.sba.gov/sites/sbagov/files/2023-06/Table%20of%20Size%20Standards_Effective%20March%2017%2C%202023%20%282%29.pdf

### 5.2 Digital maturity of local trades

48. **San Francisco is not ahead of the U.S. on websites.** RapidWebLaunch 2026: SF **82.7%** of Google-listed businesses have a website vs **83%** sample-wide; city score **72/100**. Electricians nationally in that census: **only 71.7% have a site**. Independents (the Güd Vector ICP) lag chains on analytics, HTTPS, and forms.  
    https://rapidweblaunch.com/state-of-small-business-web-design

49. **Jobber 2026 AI split by trade is a Bay Area targeting clue:** HVAC (81.5%), plumbing (65%), electrical (64%), roofing (67%) already use AI; cleaning (50%) and lawn care lag. A Bay Area HVAC/plumbing/electrical owner is more likely to *get* a custom AI pitch than a cleaner — and more likely to have already tried ChatGPT for quotes.

54. **Other NorCal trade doors (not fully profiled here):** Nextdoor neighborhood threads (BrightLocal: Facebook/Nextdoor-weighted recommendations in some AI tools — TradeWorks is secondary); local IBEW/UA halls for union shops; inspection/environmental niches (Cal/OSHA, local environmental health departments, IAQA, InterNACHI) — **no public 2025–2026 vendor-discovery survey for those niches was found.**

### What this means for Güd Vector

A Bay Area hour is worth **~$50 fully loaded at the electrician mean**, so “never miss the after-hours call” and “quotes that go out tonight” are easier to price here than in a $25-wage market. Do not assume SF shops are digitally sophisticated: **almost one in five Google-listed SF businesses still has no site**, and electricians are worse. Work **PHCC East Bay / SF-San Mateo / Redwood Empire** and **ABC NorCal** as peer-referral channels; use BBB/chamber as trust, not as the close. Speak to **owner-operators and 5–15 person HVAC/plumbing/electrical/inspection shops**, not CEA’s $30B GCs.
